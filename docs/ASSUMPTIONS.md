# Assumptions

Every value that is neither computed nor taken from a cited source (`docs/SOURCES.md`) is
listed here. Temporary values that stand in for missing entries of
`docs/ENGINEERING_INPUTS.md` are marked **TEMPORARY** and are replaced once the engineer
fills them in.

| ID | Assumption | Value | Where it is used | Why / effect on results | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | The Moon is a sphere of radius 1737.4 km; the local vertical of a site is the radial direction | 1737400 m | `ephemeris`, later `horizon` | Same reference sphere as the LOLA heights and as JPL Horizons ("Center radii 1737.4, 1737.4, 1737.4 km"), so heights and elevations are consistent. Elevation angles are relative to the horizontal plane of the sphere, not to the local terrain slope | Fixed |
| A2 | Light time and stellar aberration are evaluated at the Moon's centre and the site vector is subtracted afterwards (parallax is exact) | - | `ephemeris` | Makes one ephemeris evaluation per time serve all sites. Measured effect: agreement with Horizons stays within 0.00003 deg for polar sites and 0.0002 deg for Apollo 11 (see docs/SOURCES.md) | Fixed |
| A3 | Relativistic light bending is not modelled | - | `ephemeris` | Horizons applies it; the difference is inside the agreement quoted in A2 | Fixed |
| A4 | Angular radii use the equatorial radii of the Sun and the Earth from `pck00011.tpc`; the Earth is treated as a disc of that radius | Sun 695700 km, Earth 6378.1366 km | `ephemeris`, later `visibility` | Earth's polar flattening (about 0.3 %) changes its apparent radius by less than 0.003 deg | Fixed |
| A5 | Terrain outside the 40 m regional model (a square of +-304 km around the pole, about 80 S at the middle of each side) is ignored | - | `horizon`, `pipeline/build_horizons.py` | Tested in T19 with the 240 m model of 60-90 S as an extra layer and rays of 500 km: for all 15 polar sites and mast heights 2 m and 20 m the horizon did not change in any of the 720 azimuths (re-run on 2026-11-14 with the same result). With a 20 m mast, terrain outside the 40 m model stays below the horizon by at least 0.66 deg at Peak near Cabeus B, 0.69 deg at Malapert Massif and 1.06 deg or more at the other 13 sites (smallest margin over the 720 azimuths; horizon of the outside terrain alone, traced to 500 km, compared with the stored horizon). The farthest terrain point that defines a horizon is 256 km away (Malapert Massif, 20 m mast), inside the default ray length of 300 km. The check must be repeated for any new site north of about 84 S | Verified for the current site list |
| A6 | Terrain between pixel centres is interpolated bilinearly; the rasters are taken at face value | - | `horizon` | The 5 m `*_surf.tif` models fill pixels without laser returns by interpolation (PGDA README), so their real resolution is coarser than 5 m in places. The PGDA error and count maps are not used. Features smaller than the real data spacing (boulders, small craters) are absent from the horizon | Fixed; limitation |
| A7 | An observer height of 0 m means a point exactly on the DEM surface | - | `horizon`, mast trade-off | The horizon is then set by terrain one or two pixels away (median distance 10-50 m for most sites with a 5 m model), i.e. by pixel-level slopes that are at the noise level of the model. Results for 0 m are shown but should not be read as the performance of a real lander; 2 m is the lowest height we consider meaningful | Engineer to confirm |
| A8 | Sites without a 5 m model (Peak near Cabeus B, Mons Mouton Plateau, Slater Plain, IM-2 Athena) use the 40 m model from the first sample at 40 m | - | `horizon` | Their near field is smoother than at sites with a 5 m model, so they react less to mast height. Differences between the two groups at mast heights of a few metres partly reflect the terrain model, not the terrain | Limitation |
| A9 | Each of the 720 azimuth bins (0.5 deg) takes the maximum over 4 rays; the radial step is one pixel of the finest model up to 1000 pixels and 0.1 % of the distance beyond | - | `horizon` | A peak between two bin centres is picked up by the bin it lies in; the sampling is finite, so this is not a strict upper bound. Measured: 8 rays instead of 4 change the mean horizon of Connecting Ridge at 2 m by 0.002 deg, and a radial step of one pixel everywhere changes no azimuth by more than 0.002 deg (Connecting Ridge, Haworth, 40 m model, 150 km). `HorizonProfile.elevation_at` returns the value of the bin that contains the azimuth. The radial step can place a ridge edge late by at most one step (0.1 % of its distance); for a ridge 5 km above the observer at 100 km this lowers its elevation by about 0.005 deg | Fixed |
| A10 | The lander itself, its shadow, dust and local rocks are not modelled | - | `horizon`, `visibility` | Only terrain in the LOLA models blocks the Sun or the Earth | Limitation |
| A11 | The solar disc and the Earth's disc are uniform circles; the visible fraction is the fraction of the disc area above the terrain horizon | 32 vertical strips per disc | `visibility` | Each strip uses the horizon of the 0.5 deg azimuth bin it falls in, so a horizon that changes across the disc is followed. Not modelled: limb darkening of the Sun (the edge of the disc is darker, so a thin visible sliver gives less power than its area share), the phase of the Earth, light reflected from sunlit terrain | Fixed; limitation |
| A12 | Default rule for "sunlit": any part of the solar disc is above the horizon. Default rule for "Earth visible": the centre of the Earth's disc is above the terrain horizon, margin 0 deg | fraction > 0; margin 0 deg | `visibility.window_metrics`, later `comms` | "Any part of the disc" is the definition of the published LOLA maps and makes the numbers comparable; it is optimistic for power, so `mean_sun_fraction_pct` (average visible share of the disc) is reported next to it and the power model (T08) must use the disc fraction, not the yes/no flag. The Earth rule and margin are placeholders: `comm_earth_disk_rule` and `comm_elevation_margin_deg` in docs/ENGINEERING_INPUTS.md are still empty | Temporary; engineer to set |
| A13 | Nights and gaps are counted in whole time steps (number of consecutive samples times the step) | 1 h step in the pipeline | `visibility` | Each end of a night is uncertain by up to one step. A night that touches the start or the end of the window is flagged (`reaches_window_edge`): its real length may be larger than the value shown | Fixed |
| A14 | Lander power values are not confirmed by the team's engineer. Since 2026-11-14 (on the programmer's request) docs/ENGINEERING_INPUTS.md holds values proposed from public sources, marked [предложено]; the remaining keys fall back to `PLACEHOLDER_INPUTS` in `lunarnav.power` | From sources: cell efficiency 0.307 (Spectrolab XTJ Prime datasheet); battery 20 000 Wh, depth of discharge 0.9, 200 Wh/kg, night load 46 W (NASA GRC COMPASS reference lander, 2022). Placeholders: tracking vertical panel 1.0 m2, no extra losses, charge and discharge efficiency 0.95 each, full at landing, day load 100 W | `power`, all power results of the app, mast trade-off, landing windows | The NASA design is a small science lander that operates through the 354 h night at mid latitudes, not a south-pole lander; its battery and night load are a reference scale, not our lander. The placeholders are round numbers without a source. The app flags every unconfirmed key; verdicts and battery masses illustrate the method. `panel_losses = 0` (packing, temperature, dust, converter) is optimistic | Proposed / temporary; engineer to confirm or replace |
| A15 | Day mode (day load) only while the panel's generation covers the day load; otherwise the night (survival) load. Chosen in T23 on the programmer's request; the first rule was 'day load whenever any part of the Sun is visible' | `load_day_rule` = power_covers_load | `power.load_w`, `PowerSystem.day_load_rule` | With the first rule a thin sliver of Sun kept the 100 W day load on for many hours while the panel gave less: it made a 20 m mast at Malapert Massif need more battery than 10 m (8070 against 7084 Wh, first placeholders, T10) and made 14-day missions at Connecting Ridge fail without a single night (1362 Wh instead of 19 Wh, T11). The new rule mirrors a lander that enters survival mode when power is short; it ignores hysteresis and the cost of switching modes. The first rule is still available in the model and in the app | Proposed; engineer to confirm or change |
| A16 | Power model: constant power inside each 1 h step; solar constant 1361 W/m2 at 1 au scaled with the inverse square of the Sun distance; the panel sits at the observer height of the horizon profile (top of the mast); battery limited only by capacity and by the two efficiencies | - | `power` | Not modelled: panel and battery temperature, heater power that depends on temperature, degradation, self-discharge, charge and discharge power limits, shadow of the lander on its own panel, reflected light. A tracking panel is assumed to point exactly at the Sun's azimuth at all times | Limitation; engineer to review |
| A17 | A contact window is line of sight from the site to the Earth above the terrain horizon; nothing else is checked | Rule and margin from A12 | `comms` | Not modelled: which ground station sees the Moon at that moment, weather and station schedules, antenna pointing limits of the lander, multipath from terrain near the line of sight when the Earth is low, relay satellites. Window lengths are therefore upper limits for a direct link | Limitation; engineer to review |
| A18 | The link budget is computed only from the engineer's radio parameters; there are no placeholder values for them | - | `comms.link_parameters_from_inputs` | While any of the seven required rows of docs/ENGINEERING_INPUTS.md (frequency, transmit power, both antenna gains, system noise temperature, data rate, required Eb/N0) is empty, no link margin is shown. The range used is the distance to the Earth's centre from the ephemeris; a station on the surface is up to one Earth radius closer, about 0.15 dB | Waiting for the engineer |
| A19 | Mast heights and what the mast carries are placeholders: `mast_heights_m` and `mast_carries` in docs/ENGINEERING_INPUTS.md are empty | 0, 2, 5, 10, 20 m (list of task T10); panel and antenna both at the top of the mast | `mast`, `pipeline/mast_tradeoff.py` | The panel and the antenna see the sky from the top of the mast. If the engineer writes that the mast carries only one of them, the other stays at the lowest height of the list; with the default list that is 0 m, which A7 calls not meaningful, so the list should then start with the deck height of the lander. The mast is a point: its own shadow, sway and the lander body are not modelled (A10) | Temporary; engineer to set |
| A20 | "The gain levels off" at the lowest listed height that already gives 90 % of the largest gain among the listed heights; the gain is counted from the lowest listed height of at least 2 m (A7) | share 0.9; baseline 2 m | `mast.levelling_off_heights`, `pipeline/mast_tradeoff.py` | A description of the computed table, not an optimum: the mass, cost, deployment risk and shadow of the mast are not modelled, and nothing is known above the largest listed height. The answer can only be one of the listed heights. The 90 % share is the programmer's choice; a metric with a small total gain still gets a height (Haworth: +4.85 points of sunlit time from 2 to 20 m, levelling-off height 20 m), so the size of the gain is always reported next to it | Programmer's choice; engineer to review |
| A21 | The trade-off table of T10 covers the calendar year 2027 with 1 h steps, the battery is full at 2027-01-01 00:00 UTC | 2027-01-01 to 2028-01-01, 8760 steps | `pipeline/mast_tradeoff.py` | A year contains one full cycle of the Sun's latitude in the lunar frame: in our ephemeris the sub-solar latitude crosses the lunar equator northwards every 344.5 to 348.8 days (2026-2030) and reaches +1.547 deg on 2027-04-27, the darkest season at the south pole. The battery size is therefore the one for a whole year of operation; a short mission needs less and depends on the landing date (T11). The script takes other windows with --start and --stop | Programmer's choice; engineer to review |
| A22 | Best landing days are ranked by (1) survival of the whole mission, (2) the highest lowest state of charge during it, (3) the largest share of time with the Earth link, (4) the earlier date; days are taken from the top down and a day whose mission would overlap the mission of a day already taken is skipped | top 5; separation = mission length | `windows.best_landing_dates`, `pipeline/landing_windows.py` | Safety first, then communication. Many days can tie: at Connecting Ridge (2 m, placeholder power system) 11 landing days of 2027-2028 keep the battery full and the Earth in view for the whole 14 days, so the top 5 are the earliest non-overlapping ones among equals and the report says how many tie. Not used for the ranking: how high the Sun and the Earth stand above the terrain (a margin against errors of the terrain model), the time of day of the landing, the landing itself (approach lighting, Earth view during descent) | Programmer's choice; engineer to review |
| A23 | A mission lasts `mission_duration_days` from a landing at 00:00 UTC of the landing day, with the battery at its initial state of charge (A14: full); the length is a placeholder while the row is empty | 14 days (task T11) | `windows`, `pipeline/landing_windows.py` | One landing time per UTC day; a landing later the same day can differ by up to a day of light or link. Power, load and link rules are those of the whole tool (A12, A14, A15) | Temporary; engineer to set |

## Reference frames

Site coordinates and the LOLA terrain are in the lunar mean Earth / polar axis (ME) frame of
DE421 (`MOON_ME`). The ephemeris module uses the DE440 kernel set and the SPICE frame
`MOON_ME_DE440_ME421`, which is the DE440 lunar orientation rotated to the DE421 ME frame.

Measured with the kernels in `data/raw/kernels/` over 2027-2029 (37 epochs, 30-day step):

| Compared frames | Largest rotation | Displacement on the surface |
|---|---|---|
| `MOON_ME_DE440_ME421` vs `MOON_ME_DE421` (frame we use vs frame of LOLA) | 0.051 arcsec | 0.43 m |
| `MOON_ME_DE440_ME421` vs `MOON_PA_DE440` (ME vs principal axes) | 103.9 arcsec | 875 m |

Consequences: the frame we use and the LOLA frame agree far below the 5 m terrain pixel, so
no correction is applied. Mixing ME and PA coordinates would move a site by almost 900 m,
which matters near the pole; coordinates from sources that do not name their frame (LROC
IM-2 position, Wueller et al. dataset) are assumed to be ME, see docs/SOURCES.md. JPL
Horizons reports "MEAN_ME (high precision)" for lunar surface sites and DE441 as the
ephemeris; our DE440-based results agree with it to 0.00003 deg at the polar test sites.

Computation time (measured 2026-11-14, laptop with 12 CPU threads and 6 GB RAM, one
process): loading the 40 m regional model takes 6 s once; one site with five observer
heights, 720 azimuths x 4 rays and 300 km rays takes 4.5 s (Haworth), 4.7 s (Malapert
Massif) and 7.3 s (Connecting Ridge). The terrain along the rays is sampled once and
reused for all heights. A repeated run reproduces the stored files byte for byte.

Reading: at ridge-top sites a mast of a few metres lifts the observer above terrain that is
only tens to hundreds of metres away, and the horizon drops by 1-2 deg; this is the effect
the mast trade-off relies on, and it depends on the 5 m models being right at that scale
(A6, A7). Shackleton Rim is the centre of the PGDA raster, which lies inside the crater: its
horizon is the crater wall about 5 km away and a mast does not help. Leibnitz Beta Plateau
is also a raster centre, not a chosen landing point.
